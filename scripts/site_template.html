What “{{PCT_EXACT}} exact” actually looks like

A board counts as exact only if all 64 squares are right, so one missed pawn fails the whole photograph. That makes it a harsh headline for a pipeline that is usually one square away from perfect: {{PCT_WITHIN_ONE}} of photographs come back with at most a single square wrong, and {{PCT_WITHIN_THREE}} with at most three.

The errors are lopsided in a useful way. Of the squares that hold a piece, {{PCT_OCCUPIED}} are named correctly, and of the {{WRONG_TOTAL}} squares the pipeline gets wrong across the whole split, {{PCT_MISNAMED}} are a piece it found and then named wrongly — against {{PCT_MISSED}} it failed to see at all. Just {{N_SPURIOUS}} squares out of {{N_SQUARES}} are empty ones it wrongly filled in. When it is wrong now, it is usually confused rather than blind, and the confusion is specific: a queen called a king accounts for a sixth of every wrong square on the board.

How a photograph becomes a position

  1. 1Find the pieces

    An RT-DETR detector emits a box and a class for the board and for every piece on it — six types in two colours. It says what is in the picture, not where on the board it stands.

  2. 2Find the board

    A Swin-T heatmap model regresses the four board corners. Four corners give a homography, and a homography turns the photograph's perspective into a plain 8×8 grid.

  3. 3Assign and orient

    Each piece is placed on the square its base stands on. Square colour parity and where the pieces sit then decide which corner is a8 — the step that fails in the fourth example above.

The two models are trained separately and never see each other's output during training. The numbers on this page are end to end: the detector works from the photograph and the corner model's own predicted geometry, not from annotated corners.

None of the training images were real

Teaching a model to read a board needs far more labelled positions than anyone wants to annotate by hand, so ChessSight renders them in Blender instead — 20 000 of them, with labels that come out exact and free. Randomised piece sets, materials, lighting, camera angles and clutter; positions drawn from real Lichess games and from uniform random placement, so the model cannot lean on chess priors instead of reading pixels.

Where it breaks

  • Small pieces

    mAP 0.44 on small objects against 0.94 on the board. Distant boards and low, grazing camera angles degrade badly. Reading a position tolerates this better than the detection score suggests, because a square only needs its piece found once.

  • Orientation

    Deciding which corner is a8 is a discrete choice, and when it goes wrong the whole position is wrong at once — a perfectly read board can score 48 of 64 purely because it was read from the wrong end. Sparse endgames give it the least to work with, and are exactly where it fails.

  • Off-domain footage

    Confidences are calibrated on ChessReD-like photographs and mean progressively less as footage diverges. On small, blurred or near-edge-on boards the detector's scores saturate on people and background.

  • Tall pieces

    Queen for king is the most common single error, with rook for knight behind it. Both are pairs that differ mainly in the shape of a silhouette a few dozen pixels tall, and a crowded back rank hides most of that difference.

  • One seed

    Every figure here comes from a single training run per model. Differences smaller than about 0.02 mAP are not distinguishable from run-to-run noise.

  • Not for officiating

    Intended for analysis and research on chess imagery. Not validated for officiating, rating, or any setting where a misread board carries a cost.

Running it

# one photograph in, one FEN per line out
uv run chesssight predict photo.jpg \
  --detector runs/rtdetr_corners/best \
  --corners  runs/corner_swin_v2/best \
  --diagram  position.svg

# the same pipeline with a browser in front of it
uv run chesssight demo \
  --detector runs/rtdetr_corners/best \
  --corners  runs/corner_swin_v2/best

# render training data of your own
uv run chesssight synth run -c configs/default.yaml -n 20000 -w 2

“No board found” prints as exactly that rather than a guess. The training recipe, the per-class scores and the rest of the known limitations are in the model card.
